# D-0015 — Convert gravity at vanilla’s world-facing boundaries

**Decision:** six-way placement directions rotate vanilla’s ordered directions into
world axes. Horizontal-only blocks project the wearer’s world look onto XZ; a
vertical look falls back to the local heading’s projection, then the supporting wall.
The block’s own facing restrictions remain authoritative. Explosion impulses remain
world vectors in the explosion’s public map and packet; only the addition to stored
player velocity converts to local axes, exactly once on either side.

A name remains a camera-facing billboard, anchored beyond the head using the body’s
blended orientation. The vanilla contact blob projects onto nearby full block faces
under the wearer’s local feet, preserving its texture, brightness and distance fade.
Shader shadow maps retain the light’s direction. Ordinary players take vanilla’s path.

**Evidence:** the added real-server cases failed on ceiling placement and ceiling
explosions before the adapters changed. They now cover six axes, axial/oblique/vertical
looks, dispenser and furnace placement states, and actual level explosions starting
from zero and nonzero velocity. The real-client gravity booth covers tracked remote
frames, camera direction/distance and the actual explosion packet handler. The remote
actor is server-controlled through the real tracker, not an independently connected
second client. Captures were inspected with vanilla rendering and with Iris/Sodium
and Complementary Unbound 5.8.1 from the player’s current settings.

**Release correction:** removing armour under a ceiling could leave the standing box
inside the ceiling. A floating-point 0.1-step loop skipped the final body-height
endpoint. The release search now includes that exact endpoint using an integer step
count. A JUnit regression failed before the change; the real-server test walks each
wall onto a ceiling and removes the helmet into a collision-free world frame.

**Camera finding:** no production camera change was needed. NeoForge computes camera
angles before camera position and clipping. The rear camera follows the transformed
look: four blocks away with room, about one block at a one-block ground clearance.
Vanilla’s eight offset rays give sqrt(1 + 0.1² + 0.1²) at that clearance, explaining
the observed 1.00995 distance. The low/high scenes pin this behaviour. The older
project note claiming a rotation-order bug is superseded by these measurements.

**Trade-off:** narrow adapters preserve vanilla block and packet semantics and keep
non-wearers unaffected. The contact blob supports full blocks, as vanilla’s own
contact-shadow pass does; arbitrary voxel-face projection is outside this change.

**First-person obstruction correction:** full-booth image review caught an opaque black
wall texture despite green movement assertions. `ScreenEffectRenderer.getOverlayBlock`
sampled foot X/Z and eye Y, so a wall-walker’s support block obscured their real eyes.
The adapter now rotates vanilla’s eight probe offsets around `getEyePosition`, retaining
its block-state and visibility checks. The saved failing frame had zero bright pixels;
the fixed real-client image shows unobstructed sky. The full survival booth now asserts
that view remains visible, and the focused booth checks first-person support scenes
in every frame.

**Booth timing correction:** under Iris a strike assertion sampled one client tick before
the tracked clip arrived. Server start and client-observation traces demonstrated normal
playback immediately afterward. Keyframe photography now waits for observed playback
with a forty-client-tick timeout, then advances with the actor’s client tick count.
The face close-up camera moved nearer; its existing presence threshold was retained.
