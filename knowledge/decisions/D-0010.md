# D-0010 -- The way's search stays exact A*; it is made cheap by a rock-depth bound, a per-search table and primitives, not by a bigger budget or a looser search

**Decision**: `Burrow` keeps exact A* over cells with a consistent heuristic, a 4000-expansion
budget as a backstop and `planNearest` for a target cut off. Its bound is the taxicab
distance at the air cost plus, for each of the cells within the target's rock depth that
any way must enter, the rock cost's excess over air; the rock depth is the taxicab
distance from the target to the nearest air, looked for up to eight cells out
(`rockDepth`). One `Table` per search reads each cell from the level once and keeps, per
cell, its kind, what passing it is, the best cost known, where that came from and
whether it is closed -- open addressing on the cell packed into a long -- and the open
set is a binary heap of primitives, so an expansion is a few probes and allocates nothing.
The entity digs only while the way itself runs through rock within a strike's reach, and a
refused crawl brings the next plan forward to within five ticks rather than to the next
tick. A strike's section is the crawl's along the heading plus the tube round the way's
next cells laid into the head's face (`Tunnel.along`), nearest cells first, and only rock
with no fluid on a face is cut (`Tunnel.cuttable`). `LevelCells` reads a cell in a chunk that
is not loaded as hard: a plan never loads a chunk.

**Why**: the booth's dig had passed on a straight-line fallback the playtest fixes removed,
never on the planner: with the plain bound (taxicab times the air cost, 1, against rock at
5) a hunt to a point ten blocks into a hill spent 8000 expansions in the open and 16000
under a ceiling before committing to the rock, and a budget-exhausted plan cost 7-44 ms in
the game, each expansion reading its six neighbours and each rock neighbour the
twenty-seven about it straight from the level. The rock-depth bound is admissible and
consistent (no cell within the depth is air, so each of those costs rock at least) and
brings the same scenes to 500-2000 expansions; the table cuts the level reads about
tenfold; the primitives take the rest. Measured on synthetic cells: a found plan 0.2-1.5 ms,
an exhausted 4000-expansion search 2.4-4.2 ms; a random-rock test holds every way to the
cost a plain search of every cell finds, hunting and stalking. The budget stays 4000
because it is now two to eight times what the hard scenes need, and a bigger budget only
raises the worst case. The cheaper search then reached a far bearing through the flat
ground of the gametest world and loaded chunks on the way (1.3 s for one plan), hence the
reader's rule. And it chose an equally cheap way with a different bend through the
tunnel test's hill, where a section along the heading alone missed the bend's outer
floor cell; the head stepped up onto it and jammed under its own bore's roof, unable to
dig with the way's next cells already cut (the domain simulation in the store's notes
reproduced a narrow bend in two of six strike timings and none with the tube).

**Alternatives rejected**: *a bigger budget* -- four times the expansions at the old cost
per expansion would be 80 ms a plan; *weighted A\** -- fewer expansions, but it returns a
way up to the weight times dearer, which lets a stalker dig where a way round exists,
against what the costs say; *a search box about start and target* -- bounds the flood but
hides a way that leaves the box, and the rock-depth bound made it unnecessary; *a dense
array over a region* -- cheaper probes than the hash table, but the search is unbounded in
principle and the table's probes are not where the time goes in the game.
