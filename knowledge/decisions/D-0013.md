# D-0013 -- Hard to kill by count, not by health: the profile caps what one blow takes

**Decision**: a creature's profile says how many blows it takes to kill at the least
(`stats.blows`, five for the Face-Stealer), and the carapace never lets one blow take more
than that share of the health (`Carapace.blowCap`, health over blows, a hundredth over so the
last blow takes the last of the health in the game's float arithmetic): a blow on the crack
lands whole up to the cap, an explosion's half anywhere up to the cap, plating still clangs,
and damage that bypasses invulnerability (the void, `/kill`) is not routed at all. Health
stays 84.

**Why**: Rusty: "It should take at least 5 hearty hits even with a hard hitting weapon to kill
it." The pack's weapons hit anywhere from a wooden sword's four to a rifle's dozens; more
health would set the count for one weapon and be wrong for every other, while a cap on the
blow makes the count what the profile says whatever the weapon, and leaves ordinary weapons
needing more. The crack already asks the player to aim; the cap asks them to keep at it.

**Rejected**: raising the health until the hardest weapon in the pack needs five (wrong the
day a harder one arrives, and it makes a wooden sword hopeless); a cooldown that closes the
crack after a hit (limits hits a second, not the count); armour points (the game's formula,
opaque and never a count).

**Consequences**: the loot gametest kills with five blows of a million and checks it lives
through four; the same evening's finding that a sword never landed at all (the parts stood
only on the server; a sword's target is picked on the client) is fixed alongside, so the
count is now reachable with a sword. Whether five is right is a tuning-session number.
