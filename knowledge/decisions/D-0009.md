# D-0009 -- Gravity in six directions: the server's rule, the client's blend, the entity's motion in its own frame

**Decision**: a wearer of the full chitin set has a frame: one of six axis gravities
(`Gravity`), with right, up and forward world axes (`Frame`). Its velocity, its look and
its box are local to that frame; the mixins on the entity turn them to the world at the
edges: the box is the axis swap with the feet on the gravity face, the eyes are up along
the frame, the view vector is the local look turned, the move collides the frame's up
axis first and steps up along it, the supporting block is under the frame's feet;
`travel` is untouched except that its calls to `move` are turned local-to-world. The
frame changes by pure `Transition` rules (into a wall when pushing at 0.02+ and the box
fits; over an edge onto the ledge's face; release to the world's down by the least way
out) that the server applies on `PlayerTickEvent.Post` from its own copy and the client
applies to its own player a tick ahead; the frame rides one synced byte on the player,
no packet added. The client blends the camera and the model over six eased ticks. Every
hook returns at once for the world's frame, so non-wearers run vanilla byte for byte.

**Why**: Rusty chose full reorientation ("gravity, camera roll, model on the wall"). Six
axis directions and not an arbitrary vector because collision is boxes against voxel
shapes: block faces are the only surfaces, and a box on a wall is an axis swap, which an
arbitrary vector cannot be (safe from bugs, easy to understand). A wearer's velocity is
local by definition, so gravity, jumping and friction need no change; the server's
re-run of a reported move goes through the same move, so one deterministic rule on both
sides keeps them agreeing within the game's tolerance. The wall is read off the game's
own move for a wearer still in the world's frame, so the transition needs no second
collision pass.

**Alternatives rejected**: *arbitrary gravity vectors* -- oriented boxes the engine
cannot collide; *a custom packet for the frame* -- a synced byte is fewer moving parts;
*a client-only trick* -- the server must own the box, or every hit and every "moved
wrongly" would disagree; *a climbing flag on the vanilla mover* -- no ceilings, no
roll, not what was asked.
