# D-0003 -- Feet are planted on the level by a pure rule, nothing synced; clips are started by the server by name

**Decision**: each leg's foot is a value (`Legs.Foot`: planted at an anchor, swinging
from one to another, or hanging) stepped once a tick by the pure `Legs.step` over a
`Cells` view of the blocks (`LevelCells` on either side): a planted foot stays until it
is a stride from its rest point or its block is gone, then swings on a lifted arc to the
surface under the rest point led along the travel; a foot may start only while its
pair's other stands and fewer than 40 % of all feet swing, in the gait's metachronal
order, hanging feet first; swings shorten (4, 3, 2 ticks) and the lead grows with speed
so the feet keep up with a scurry. Each leg's segment, hip and rest foot come off the
file (`Body.of`: the nearest chain ancestor, the pivots, the farthest vertex of the
leg's cubes as the file stands them). Neither the feet nor the chain pose is synced;
both sides step from the trail and blocks they already have. Authored clips
(`FaceStealerClips`) are started on the server (`Aberrant.play`) and named over synced
data with a serial; each side advances its own `Animator`; the server acts on the cues.

**Why**: Rusty: "Most legs must be in contact with some stable surface at all times, but
not all of them. It should have a metachronal gait." A sinusoidal gait floats the feet
through the ground; anchors on real blocks do not. Syncing 26 feet or 12 segments every
tick costs bandwidth for nothing: both sides can compute the same thing, and a one-tick
divergence is invisible while hits are judged on the server's parts. A clip by name is
one string and one int; a clip's turns are the same on every client by construction.
The lag was found in the crawl test: at 0.3 blocks a tick with 4-tick swings and a 40 %
cap, twelve legs each step every twelve ticks and fall 3.5 blocks behind; the swing and
the lead scale with speed rather than the cap, which keeps Rusty's "most down".

**Alternatives rejected**: *syncing the feet* -- 26 vectors a tick a creature; *a higher
swing cap at speed* -- breaks "most legs down"; *inverse kinematics per leg* -- the leg is
one bone in the file; a lever (lift in the leg's plane, then yaw) aims it well enough
and a two-bone leg can come with child bones later; *clips as entity data per bone* --
the clip is code on both sides, only its start needs saying.
